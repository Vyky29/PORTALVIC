Important — CQC scope: Care is delivered at our Day Centre (typically ~5-hour sessions), not in the person’s own home. Under CQC guidance, the regulated activity of Personal care applies when care is provided where the person is living. Standalone day centres that provide changing / personal care on site are generally outside CQC registration. Do not submit a domiciliary care application describing home-based personal care unless that service will actually be delivered. This document describes our real Day Centre model for NHS / families / quality assurance.

2.What we actually deliver

People attend our Day Centre for structured day sessions (typically around 5 hours). They come to us; we do not deliver a domiciliary / home-care visiting service as the core model.

During the session, depending on individual need and the agreed plan, support may include:

  • Continuous adult supervision (often 1:1 or 2:1)
  • Personal care on site — e.g. changing, toileting support, post-activity shower / hygiene
  • Medication support only if agreed in the plan and staff are competent (prompting or administration as authorised)
  • Structured activities, sensory / Hub regulation, transitions, snacks
  • Progress reporting for families and commissioners (including NHS)

3.Aims and objectives

  • Safe, predictable, person-centred day support for autistic people and people with learning disabilities / SEN
  • Dignity in personal care and hygiene support during the day session
  • Accessible communication and Positive Behaviour Support approaches
  • Partnership with families, NHS and local authorities
  • Trained workforce with enhanced DBS and ongoing competence

7.If the NHS asks for “CQC registration”

Explain the real model: day attendance with on-site personal care. Ask whether they need:

  1. Written confirmation that day services are outside CQC scope; plus our quality pack (policies, ISPs, incidents, training), or
  2. A future additional domiciliary personal care service delivered in people’s homes (only then pursue CQC Path B).

Registering as a domiciliary agency while only delivering centre-based care would be inaccurate and risks rejection or regulatory action.
